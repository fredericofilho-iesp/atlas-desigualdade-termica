Who is most exposed to heat?

An open atlas to examine how exposure to extreme heat is distributed across space and how it relates to income, race, age and urban infrastructure, at four scales.

Three dimensions, analysed separately

Exposure (land surface temperature, air temperature, extreme heat days), sensitivity (age, income, race, housing conditions) and adaptive capacity (vegetation, sanitation, access to water). Heat inequality is not only a difference in temperature: a hot area may have the resources to protect itself, while a cooler area may concentrate highly vulnerable people.

Principles

  • Land surface temperature (satellite) and air temperature (reanalysis) are different measures and are never mixed.
  • Every published indicator states its source, period, unit and aggregation method.
  • Area-level correlations do not support causal conclusions about individuals.
  • Scripts and processed data are kept in the repository for reproducibility.

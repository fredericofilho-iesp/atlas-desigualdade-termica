How to read: municipal values are built from Brazil’s roughly 450,000 census tracts and weighted by people, so temperature is measured where people live. Surface temperature comes from MODIS (1 km, early afternoon) and refers to the hottest three months of each place: in much of the Centre-West, North and Northeast the peak is the dry season (August–October), not the rainy summer. At 1 km the map compares municipalities and regions; the city and state maps use 30 m Landsat images for neighbourhood detail. The “heat gap” indicators show inequality inside each municipality (red: the poorer group, or Black and brown residents, lives in hotter places; blue: in cooler ones) and are left blank in small municipalities, where they would be unstable.

Planned sources

MODIS/Aqua MYD11A2 v6.1 (NASA, via Microsoft Planetary Computer); IBGE, 2022 Census (tract boundaries, income, color or race, age, street trees); municipal boundaries via geobr (Ipea). Details in Methodology.
